Conceptual instrument, not a forecast. The map is curated by hand: which places count as having had a peaceful revolution is contested, contestable, and editable at the top of the source. Each entry links to Wikipedia as a starting point — dig deeper from there. Peoples and de-facto or disputed polities that are not UN members (diamond markers) are included as first-class participants and counted — legitimacy here follows those present, not those recognized. Situations marked developing (blue) are unfolding now and are deliberately not counted until they cross the line. Coups (blue) and wars (red) are shown to illustrate the state of the world but are not counted toward the peaceful-revolution threshold — a military coup is not a peaceful revolution. Places marked recent improvement (blue) — a ceasefire, the fall of a dictator, a fragile transition — are turns for the better but also not counted, since they are not (yet) peaceful revolutions. The United Kingdom has its own fractal page →. The 51% “Charter rewrite” is an act of imagination about participatory, citizen-driven governance, not a legal claim.
